A small **12 V DC Tesla coil** built for NISER's outreach Open Day to show high-frequency wireless lighting. It reliably got a "how?!" from visitors.

## How it works

A transistor oscillator on a breadboard drives the primary of a resonant transformer wound on a tube. The secondary, a tall coil topped with a metal plate, rings at a high frequency and sets up a strong, rapidly changing electric field around it. Anything that conducts gases nearby, like a fluorescent tube, lights up without touching the circuit.

## Why it worked as outreach

It takes a pocket-sized battery supply, is safe to demonstrate at 12 V input, and the tube glows when someone holds it in their own hand, which no diagram of electromagnetism can match.
